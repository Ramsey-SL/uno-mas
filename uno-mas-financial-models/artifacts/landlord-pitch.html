The Monroe Street Partnership

One loan replaces six. One vacant room becomes a second business. Uno Más already has the kitchen, the staff, the licenses, and the marketing — what's missing is the structure to put them to work for both of us.

The proposal in one sentence

Pledge the building as collateral on a single 10-year SBA loan that retires every dollar of debt currently attached to Uno Más, and become an equal partner in turning the empty unit next door into a staffed, licensed, marketed event venue — no hiring, no liquor license, no operating burden on your side.

01

Where things stand today

Uno Más carries debt across six separate facilities — two SBA term loans, a revolving line, two credit cards, and the tenant-improvement balance billed inside the lease. Different rates, different maturities, different lenders. It works, but it's fragile, and every one of those facilities is a separate point of risk for a tenant you depend on for rent.

Separately, the adjoining unit has been difficult to lease as a standalone space. Turning it into an event venue solves the vacancy — but running an event space means a manager, a caterer, a bar program, liquor licensing, and a marketing engine to fill the calendar. That's a business in itself, and not one either of us wants to build from scratch.

Uno Más already has all of it — a kitchen with wholesale food and beverage accounts, a liquor license, an operations team on-site every day, and a marketing system already promoting the building. The event space isn't a new venture. It's an extension of one that already exists next door.

What a sale means beyond the number

  • A new, stronger tenant. Whoever buys the business signs a fresh lease directly with you — a better-capitalized operator than the one paying rent today, on terms you negotiate at that time.
  • No debt follows the building. The consolidated loan is retired at closing. You keep the real estate, free of the obligation you collateralized.
