# Taco Tuesday — name alternatives

**Ramsey 2026-08-25:** *"Taco Tuesday isn't landing for me, give me other options."*

---

## First, the honest trade

**"Taco Tuesday" has one enormous advantage: zero explanation cost.** Everybody already knows what
it means. Any replacement has to be *taught*, and you'd be teaching it 52 times a year.

**It also has one real problem: it's not yours.** Every Mexican restaurant in Spokane runs one. Uno
Más is a distinctive brand with a distinctive voice, and "Taco Tuesday" is the single most generic
thing on your calendar. **Big F'N Thursday** is unmistakably you. Tuesday isn't.

**And the switching cost is real.** It's live on the homepage tile, in an SMS that went out today, in
the Meta ads you launched, on the printed table tents, and across ~30 repo files. Renaming means one
propagation pass plus reprinting collateral.

**Recommendation: only rename if the new name is clearly better, not merely different.** A lateral
move costs you the recognition you already have.

---

## Option 1 — Keep the day, change the frame
*Tuesday stays about tacos; the name gets your voice.*

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-for Tuesday** | The offer *is* BOGO, so the name states the deal instead of the category. Alliterative, instantly clear, and it survives if the food changes. | Generic-adjacent; some bars use it for drinks. |
| **Tuesday, Doubled** | Same idea, drier delivery — closer to your Tom Segura register than a pun. | Needs the offer beside it to land. |
| **Buy One Tuesday** | Says the mechanic in three words. Plain and confident. | Least fun of the three. |
| **One More Tuesday** | Ties to the brand's own meaning — *uno más* is literally "one more". **This is the only option here that comes out of your own name.** | Slightly abstract until you've seen the offer once. |

## Option 2 — Own the item, not the day
*Follows the Big F'N Thursday pattern: name the food, put the day second.*

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Street Taco Tuesday** | One word added and it's already more specific — these are *street* tacos, and BOGO applies to those only. Also **kills the "which tacos?" question at the table.** | Still contains "Taco Tuesday". |
| **Dos Tacos Tuesday** | Bilingual, on-brand, and states the quantity. Reads well spoken. | "Dos" plus "Tuesday" mixes languages — deliberate, but check you like it. |

## Option 3 — Break the pattern entirely
*Highest risk, highest distinctiveness.*

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Get Lost Tuesdays** | Straight from **"Get a little lost"** — your actual campaign platform. Ties the weekly ritual to the brand line, and no one else can use it. | Says nothing about tacos or the offer; needs the subhead to carry all the information. |
| **The Tuesday Ritual** | Leans into the habit framing you already picked for the ads (option C, "your Tuesday already has a plan"). | Grand for a taco deal. |
| **Second Taco's Free** | The most honest headline available. It's not a name, it's the offer — which for a weekly recurring deal might be the point. | Not a brandable programme name. |

---

## What I'd actually do

**1 · If you want one word of change: `Street Taco Tuesday`.** Keeps every ounce of recognition,
adds real specificity, and answers a question staff will otherwise get asked all night. Cheapest
meaningful improvement on the list.

**2 · If you want it to be *yours*: `One More Tuesday`.** It's the only option that comes out of
**Uno Más** itself — the name means "one more," the offer *is* one more taco, and the brand line is
"get a little lost." That's three things pointing the same direction, which is what a good programme
name does. It needs teaching for a few weeks, then it's yours forever and no competitor can take it.

**3 · Don't pick `Two-for Tuesday`.** It's the obvious answer, which is why several other bars in
town already are it. You'd trade a generic name for a slightly different generic name.
