WorksThe progress line solves the real problem with any rotator — nobody knows something is coming. It makes the bar do two jobs at once: name the other offer, and show it's about to arrive. 7s clears the read time on the longest tile. Cheap to build: one div and a CSS keyframe.
Costs youSlightly busier bar. And the progress line has to reset cleanly on pause and on manual dot clicks, or it desyncs from the slide and looks broken — that's the one piece worth testing properly.
Three things I'd want you to know before we build this.

1. Slide two gets far less attention than slide one. That's the consistent finding on rotating banners — most people scroll before the second slide arrives. So the ordering isn't cosmetic: whatever needs to be seen goes first, permanently. I've put the limited-time promo there because it expires. That's also the argument for the flipping bar — even a guest who never sees slide two still reads both offers, because the bar carried the other one the whole time.

2. Accessibility requires the pause control, not as a nicety — auto-moving content that runs more than five seconds needs a way to stop it. It's built in above, and rotation is disabled entirely under prefers-reduced-motion, where both offers just render stacked and static.

3. The two tiles are different heights, so the slot is locked to the taller one and the shorter tile sits with extra space rather than the page jumping every 7 seconds. You'll see that gap on the Thursday slide. Worth keeping collision-day tiles close in length for that reason.
Still the same structural note. A rotator needs one component that knows both offers exist and decides the order. That's a slot component reading from site_events — the three day tiles can't coordinate this between themselves, because none of them knows the others are there.
