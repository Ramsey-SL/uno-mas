> **Status:** Approved ✅

## The Idea

**Campaign idea:**
Use the sheer surprise of what the dinner menu actually is — Surf & Turf, oysters, a $129 Feast — to make Spokane feel like they've been missing something, and now it's time to go.

**Campaign line:**
> *"Dinner. At Uno Más."*

**Why this concept:**
The entire campaign lives in the juxtaposition. Everyone in Spokane knows Uno Más for tacos — so "Dinner. At Uno Más." creates instant intrigue because it doesn't match what they think they know. The pull isn't "here's a new menu." The pull is *"wait — really?"* Every execution leads with that reveal and lets the food (Surf & Turf, raw bar, the Feast) close the argument. This is completely uncontested positioning in Spokane's dining market.

---

## Creative Direction

**Visual direction:**
Low-light, warm amber and copper tones — the speakeasy atmosphere. Real food shot at the bar, not in a studio. Intimate close-ups of the Surf & Turf and oysters. Black negative space dominant. The vibe of a friend's photo that looked incredible. Editorial, not advertising. The space is present — not just the food.

**Tone for this campaign:**
Confidence dial turned up — still unmistakably Uno Más group-chat energy, but the subject matter has earned a grown-up moment. Intrigue first. Food does the proving. No apologies for the price. No explaining. Assertive. The warmth is there but plays second.

---

## Non-Negotiables

Every single execution must include these. No exceptions.

- [ ] Lead with the surprise or revelation — never the announcement
- [ ] Name at least one specific menu item with its price (the price is the hook)
- [ ] Drive to the dinner menu page or "get in here" CTA
- [ ] Campaign line "Dinner. At Uno Más." present or clearly echoed

---

## Guardrails

**Brand NEVER (hard failures):**
- Street tacos ❌ | Authentic Mexican ❌ | Mouthwatering ❌ | Culinary journey ❌
- Artisanal ❌ | Mixology ❌ | Amazing (generic) ❌ | Adjective stacking ❌
- "We apologize for…" ❌ | "We're excited to announce" ❌

**Angles to avoid:**
- Grand opening / launch announcement energy ❌
- Apologist framing ("a taco shop that also does dinner") ❌
- Overexplaining the menu — let the specifics speak ❌
- Fine-dining positioning language ❌

## Execution Notes by Channel

### Meta Ads (Feed + Story)
The juxtaposition is the hook. Lead visually with the food (Surf & Turf plated, oysters on ice), then the copy lands the reveal. Three distinct angles across variants: price contrast ("$47 at a taco shop"), the item revelation ("Oysters. At Uno Más. Yes, really."), and the social FOMO ("This has been happening. Your move."). CTA: "See the menu" or "Get in here."

### Email (Klaviyo — Uno Más Rewards: The Cantina Club list)
Subject line must create the curiosity gap — not announce. "So we did a thing" energy. Full-bleed food hero. Short body — 3–4 sentences max. One price anchor. One menu item. One CTA. The email should read like a text from the restaurant, not a newsletter from a brand.

### Organic Social (Instagram)
Launch post: the full reveal. Multiple menu items in a carousel or single strong hero shot. Caption: 1–2 lines. "Dinner. At Uno Más." can anchor the caption directly. Let the visual and the specifics do the work. Hashtags secondary.

## Audience Reminder

**Who:** Spokane locals/transplants, 25–45, experience planners — already know and like Uno Más
**Journey stage:** Consideration → Decision
**The belief to create:** "Uno Más isn't just for tacos — it's a legitimate dinner destination"
**Psychological lever:** Pull — the surprise of the menu IS the hook
**Their language:** "You have to go," "it's a whole vibe," "I didn't expect it to be that good," "it's like nothing else in Spokane"
