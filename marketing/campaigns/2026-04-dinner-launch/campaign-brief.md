## 2. Audience Brief

**Primary Segment**: Spokane locals and transplants, ages 25–45 — the experience planner in the group chat. Already aware of Uno Más as a taco spot; needs to be repositioned as a dinner destination.

**Journey Stage**: Consideration → Decision
> Most of this audience knows Uno Más exists. The campaign's job is to shift their mental model — from "taco shop" to "my new dinner spot." This is a perception campaign as much as a traffic campaign.

**The job they're hiring this campaign to do**:
> "When I want a real dinner out — somewhere with a vibe, impressive food, and no stuffy fine-dining commitment — I want to know Uno Más has what I need, so I can confidently pick it for tonight."

**Key psychological lever**: **Pull**
> The dinner menu creates desire. Surf & Turf, a Raw Bar, a $129 Feast — this isn't what people expect from a taco shop. The surprise IS the hook. Lead with the revelation, not the reassurance.

**The belief this campaign must create**:
> "Uno Más isn't just for tacos anymore — it's a legitimate dinner destination and the most interesting place to eat in Spokane right now."

**Audience exclusions**: Price-first decision makers, fast food seekers, anyone outside Spokane metro / reasonable Monroe Street drive.

## 3. Messaging Territory

**Core Message**:
> Uno Más just became Spokane's best dinner spot — and most people don't know it yet.

**Support Messages**:
- The food hits different: Surf & Turf, Raw Bar, Carne Asada, a $129 Feast. This isn't what you expected from us.
- Same vibe you love, leveled up. No reservations required, no dress code — just a great dinner.
- Soft launch is done. The people who tried it loved it. Now it's your turn.

**Proof Point to Lead With**:
> Soft launch validation — "people love it." Pair with visual evidence: the food itself. Quarter-pound legacy + new dinner plates = credibility bridge.

**Competitive Angle**:
> vs. fine dining: All the food quality, none of the stiffness — and you don't need a reservation two weeks out.
> vs. "taco shop" perception: This isn't a taco shop that added some dinner items. This is a dinner destination that happens to also make Spokane's best tacos.

**Tone for This Campaign**:
> Confident, slightly elevated — but unmistakably Uno Más. The voice stays group-chat energy. The subject matter earns a grown-up moment. Think: "So we did a thing… and it involves oysters." Lead with intrigue and let the menu speak for itself. No adjective stacking. No "culinary journey."

**Angles to Avoid**:
- "Authentic" anything ❌
- Fine dining comparison language (don't position as upscale — position as surprising) ❌
- Generic food photography captions ("delicious," "mouthwatering") ❌
- Overexplaining the menu — let the photos and price anchors do the work ❌
