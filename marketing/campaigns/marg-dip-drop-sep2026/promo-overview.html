Before we build

Four things we need settled

Nothing here is live and nothing is printed. Two of these change the offer itself.

Dip choice — guac and queso, or include salsa?
Recommend guacamole and queso blanco only. Salsa is the lightest of the three and does not carry the offer — guac and queso are what people actually want, and keeping it to two makes the choice easy to say out loud.
Late Night carve-out — confirm it goes on the piece
Recommend yes, and it is not really optional. Late Night Happy Hour runs Fri and Sat 8–10pm with its own margarita pricing, and the two offers work against each other in that window. Without the line the floor has no rule and will improvise. It is on the poster, the tile and the email as drafted.
Chips & Queso — verify on both printed menus
The Lunch and Dinner PDFs already disagree with each other on the Chip & Dip Trio, so it is worth a look at the individual dips before anything goes to print. Nothing on this piece quotes a menu price, but the floor still needs to ring it correctly.
Fourth bundle in five weeks — is that deliberate?
Tests 2, 3 and now 5 are all bundles. The gift card was the one structurally different test, and it is the one you just told me worked. Not an objection — but if the Drop becomes “there is always a bundle,” the novelty that made it land wears off.
Afterwards

What we learn from it

Four tests have run and none has a number attached. That is the real gap, not the creative.

  • Average check Wednesday–Sunday against the same days the week beforeThe number that says whether it worked
  • Redemption countHow many actually ordered it
  • Attach rateDid they add the dip, or swap it for something they would have ordered anyway
  • Which dip they pickedDecides whether guac or queso leads the creative next time
The Toast dashboard is the tool for this, but its API credentials currently fail authentication — so this will be a manual pull unless that gets fixed first. Worth an hour before Wednesday.
