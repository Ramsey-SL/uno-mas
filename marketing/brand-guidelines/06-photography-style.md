# Uno Más — Photography Style

## Overview

Uno Más photography should feel real, warm, and specific. Not styled-to-death. Not filtered into oblivion. The goal is to make someone hungry, curious, or envious — ideally all three at once.

The Mezzanine photography operates under a completely different visual language: dark, dramatic, atmospheric.

---

## Uno Más Photography Style

### The Feeling
Warm, candid, approachable. Like a photo a friend took at dinner — but a friend who knows what they're doing. Natural light where possible. Real people, real moments, real food.

### Food Photography

**What works:**
- **Hero shot:** Item centered, clean background, shot from slightly above (30–45°) or directly overhead
- **Action shot:** Taco being picked up, cheese pull, sauce pour, hands in frame — these outperform static shots on social
- **Flat lay:** Full spread — tacos + side + drink + chips — tells the whole story of a visit
- **Close-up detail:** One ingredient, one texture, one color moment (cotija crumble, charred edge, consomé surface)
- **The full order:** What it looks like when it arrives at the table — the "what did you get?" shot

**Lighting:** Natural light is preferred. Window light creates warmth. Avoid harsh flash — it kills texture. Avoid cool/blue light tones — this is a warm food brand.

**Backgrounds:** Clean wooden table, slate, or neutral surface. Avoid busy tablecloths or visually noisy backgrounds that compete with the food.

**Composition:** Subject fills at least 60% of the frame. Nothing blurry in the center of interest. Slight negative space on one side works well for caption overlay.

**Orientation preferences:**
- Portrait (4:5 or 9:16) for Instagram feed and Stories
- Square (1:1) acceptable for feed
- Landscape (16:9) for email headers and website banners

---

### Drink Photography

**What works:**
- **Hero cocktail shot:** Glass centered, clean background — the drink is the subject, nothing competes
- **The pour:** Action shot — liquid in motion, condensation on glass, citrus being squeezed
- **Cocktail / specials setup:** Two drinks on a bar or table together, relaxed setting, real atmosphere
- **Frozen Margarita close-up:** Color and texture if a rotating flavor is featured

**Lighting:** Same warm tone as food. Cocktails look best in warm ambient light — avoid neon-adjacent color casts unless it's a specific creative choice.

**Garnishes:** Always in frame if they're part of the drink. The lime on the Paloma, the salt rim on the margarita — these details are part of the story.

---

### Venue / Atmosphere Photography

**What works:**
- Main floor during active service — candid energy, not posed or empty
- Bar area during service — the vibe, not a product shot
- The patio (seasonal) — table setup, string lights in background, the feel of being there
- Behind the bar — hands working, action framing, not a face shot
- The TACOS neon sign — always works as a background element or focal point

**What doesn't work:**
- Empty restaurant shots — they read as sad, not inviting
- Overly posed "we're a restaurant!" stock photo energy
- Interior shots with unflattering overhead lighting
- Any space that isn't clean and ready

---

### Team Photography

**Shoot monthly. Guidelines:**
- Behind-the-scenes prep: plating, prepping, real moments — not staged
- The team at service: candid, never posed, real interactions
- One person doing one thing: specific and real (hands plating, pouring, chopping)
- Never post faces without explicit permission from the team member

## The Mezzanine Photography Style

### The Feeling
Dark. Dramatic. Intimate. The Mezzanine is a speakeasy — the photography should feel like you're in on a secret. Shadows are as important as light sources.

### Key Subjects to Shoot (Monthly or Before Events)

- **The fireplace:** Flames, leather seating, low light — no people necessary for these shots
- **Bar setup:** The upstairs bar styled and ready — bottles, glassware, atmosphere
- **Full room:** Before an event — showing the space lit and ready
- **Event in progress:** Group energy, candid, tasteful — no identifiable faces without permission
- **The lounge area:** Leather chairs, low tables, the feel of the room

### Lighting
Low, warm, directional. No overhead lighting. Use fireplace light, lamp light, and bar ambient. Deep shadows are part of the composition, not something to correct in post.

### Color Treatment
Images can lean darker and slightly desaturated in post — Electric Pink accents pop better against cooler tones. The contrast between dark backgrounds and Electric Pink/warm elements is the visual signature.

### What to Avoid for Mezzanine
- Bright, flat, evenly lit photography (that's Uno Más main floor energy)
- Outdoor or natural light photography
- Cheerful, high-energy action shots
- Empty glass or empty table hero shots (the space sells the Mezzanine, not props alone)

## Technical Standards

| Setting | Guideline |
|---|---|
| File format | Shoot RAW + JPEG when possible |
| RAW files | Store in `_unsorted/RAW/` — not for immediate use |
| JPEG files | Use for social, email, Canva — ready to use |
| Resolution | Minimum 1080px wide for digital use |
| Orientation | Portrait (9:16) for Stories/Reels, Square (1:1) or Portrait (4:5) for feed |
| Naming | `YYYYMMDD_[BRAND]_[CATEGORY]_[SUBJECT]_[VERSION].[ext]` |

### Video Technical Notes
- Reels: 15–30 seconds — first 3 seconds must hook, no slow builds
- Stories: 15 seconds max per card
- Shoot vertical (9:16) for all social video
- No shaky cam — use a surface or phone grip
- Natural sound works well (sizzle, pour, ambient) — no music needed unless licensed
