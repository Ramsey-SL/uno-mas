# Uno Más — Voice and Tone

## The Core Voice

Uno Más sounds like a confident friend texting you about where to go tonight. The reference point is **Tom Segura energy** — dry, self-aware, never trying too hard, never performing.

The brand has something real to offer. It doesn't need to announce it. The confidence is in the specificity, not the adjectives.

---

## The Three Voice Pillars

### 1. Self-Aware Confidence
The food is good. The cocktails are good. The space is good. We know it. We don't need a press release. We lead with the thing itself — the ingredient, the dish, the experience — and let it do the talking.

This means: no superlatives. No "best in Spokane" unless a third party said it. No "you'll love it." Just the real thing, named specifically.

### 2. Group Chat Energy
Every post, caption, and subject line should be worth sending to a friend. If someone reads a caption and thinks "I need to send this to my group chat," that's a win. If they'd never share it, it's probably too safe, too corporate, or too generic.

Write like someone who texts in lowercase sometimes. Write things that get screenshotted. The goal is shareability through specificity, not through trying to be funny.

### 3. Controlled Chaos
Uno Más is fun, a little unpredictable, and community-first. The WTF Menu is literally named for this energy. But "chaotic" doesn't mean unpolished — it means surprising within a confident, well-run framework.

The food is unexpected. The vibe is welcoming. The writing should feel like both.

## Tone Calibration by Context

The voice stays consistent, but the **tone** shifts depending on what we're writing:

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Food posts | Specific, confident, dry | "Braised chuck. Melted Monterey Jack. Consomé for dipping." |
| Weekly specials | Direct, time-aware, slightly playful | "Big F’N Thursday — $10 Big F’N Quesadilla. $10 tequila cocktails. Get here." |
| Mezzanine | Atmospheric, mysterious, less-is-more | "Most people don't know the Mezzanine exists. That's kind of the point." |
| Events / catering | Warm, professional, clear | "Three packages. One very good room. DM us to start planning." |
| Review responses | Personal, genuine, warm | "Maria, this one made the whole team smile." |
| Email subject lines | Direct or curiosity-forward | "Big F’N Thursday. $10 BFQ, $10 tequila cocktails." |
| SMS | Urgent, specific, no fluff | "Taco Tuesday: $6 margs. Come in. 🍹" |

---

## The Mezzanine Voice Distinction

The Mezzanine uses the same core voice but leans harder on **atmosphere over action**. It's less "come in now" and more "you should know this exists."

- Less urgency, more discovery
- Fewer adjectives, more scene-setting
- One detail does more work than a feature list
- The secret-handshake energy is part of the brand

**Uno Más:** "The Espresso Margarita is waiting. Come get a little lost tonight."  
**Mezzanine:** "Fireplace. Private bar. Most people don't know it's up there."

---

## Voice Rules

- Write in active voice always
- Short sentences land harder than long ones
- One idea per sentence when possible
- Specifics beat adjectives every time: "braised chuck" beats "tender, flavorful meat"
- The period is a tool. Use it. Even in fragments.
- Read every caption aloud. If it sounds like a restaurant marketing account, rewrite it.

---

## What This Voice Is Not

- Not corporate: "We are thrilled to announce..."
- Not pleading: "Don't forget about us!"
- Not generic food-brand: "Mouthwatering flavors await!"
- Not performatively casual: "Heyyyy y'all!!!! 🙌🙌🙌"
- Not over-explained: "The Birria Tacos are a Mexican-inspired dish that features..."
- Not trying too hard to be cool: Just be specific. The cool takes care of itself.

---

## Quick Test

Before publishing anything, ask: **"Would I send this to a friend?"**

If yes — publish.  
If no — find the one specific thing that makes it worth sending, lead with that, and cut everything else.
