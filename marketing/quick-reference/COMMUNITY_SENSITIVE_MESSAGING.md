# Community-Sensitive Messaging — Quick Reference

> **Source:** Aug 2026 handoff. Precedent set during the **Spokane wildfire conditions,
> Aug 6–7 2026**, when marketing had to acknowledge community conditions while still
> communicating that Uno Más was open and Sunday brunch was running.

## The principle

Acknowledge the situation **without appearing opportunistic**, and still give people the practical
information they need: *are you open, and what's available.*

Silence reads as tone-deaf. A promo blast reads as opportunistic. The move is a short, human,
useful message.

## What to do

1. **Lead with the community, not the offer.** One honest line about what's happening locally.
2. **State the operational facts plainly** — open/closed, hours, what's running, air-quality or
   patio-status if relevant.
3. **Drop or soften the promotional CTA.** A reservation link is fine. "Spend $60, get $10 off" is not.
4. **Keep it short.** This is not a storytelling moment.
5. **Pause scheduled sends and social posts** that would land wrong. Check Vista Social's queue.
6. **Don't claim a role you don't have** — no implied first-responder framing, no charity halo
   unless something real is actually being done.

## What not to do

- No fake scarcity, no urgency language, no "escape the smoke — come drink!" framing.
- No emoji-heavy tone.
- Don't tie a discount to the event.
- Don't go quiet for days and then resume promos with no acknowledgement.

## Applies to

Wildfire and air quality · snow/ice closures · power outages · road or Monroe-corridor
construction · local tragedy · anything where "business as usual" copy would land badly.

## Voice note

The house voice is playful and a little chaotic. **In these moments, drop the chaos and keep the
warmth.** Short sentences, direct, no jokes. Still recognizably Uno Más — just quieter.
